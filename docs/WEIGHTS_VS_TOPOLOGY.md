# Where the geometry actually lives: decomposing m_ij

Result note, 10 September 2026. Test of the claim that this circuit's feedforward geometry
resides in the weights rather than the topology, run against the CN3 poster's factorisation.

---

## 1. The poster's formula is already the decomposition

```
m_ij = q_i · c_ij · g_ij · (V_ref − V_rev) · τ_ij · κ_j
```

`q_i` depends only on the presynaptic type and `κ_j` only on the postsynaptic type. Everything
else is genuinely pairwise. So in the analysis convention `M[post, pre]` the operator is a
**two-sided diagonal scaling of a base synaptic matrix**:

```
M[post, pre]  =  diag(κ) · Bᵀ · diag(q)          B = c ⊙ g ⊙ ΔV ⊙ τ
```

That structure matters, because two-sided diagonal scaling is precisely the operation that
manufactures non-normality out of a balanced substrate. With `B` symmetric and `q = κ`,
`diag(κ) B diag(q)` is symmetric and therefore normal. Non-normality is generated by the
**mismatch between how much a population sends (`q`) and how much it responds (`κ`)**, layered
on whatever directional asymmetry sits in `c_ij`.

The neural mass model is not a neutral container for the connectome. Its own scaling terms are
candidate sources of the geometry.

---

## 2. Test

Five operators, each normalised independently to ρ = 0.95, each turned into `J = −I + W`, each
given its own weighted feedback-arc ordering (greedy net-outflow start plus best-insertion
sweeps). `peak` is max over t of ‖exp(Jt)‖₂; `fwd weight` is the fraction of total |weight|
placeable below the diagonal.

| Operator | Henrici | ω(J) | peak amp | E→I / I→E | fwd weight |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **M — real** | 99.3% | 29.58 | **27.13×** | 912 | **0.995** |
| binary — sign(M) only | 24.4% | 0.07 | **1.06×** | 1.44 | **0.716** |
| M / κ_j — no postsynaptic gain | 80.5% | 4.69 | 7.74× | 85.8 | 0.982 |
| M / q_i — no census weighting | 45.1% | 0.89 | 1.45× | 11.9 | 0.936 |
| M / (κ_j q_i) — neither | 35.9% | 0.33 | 1.13× | 0.96 | 0.916 |

Population sizes: median `q_i` is 5,615 for excitatory types and 333 for inhibitory —
**16.9× more excitatory cells per type.** Postsynaptic gain: median `κ_j` 0.0345 Hz/pA for
principal targets, 0.0935 for interneuron targets, ~9× at the tails.

### Three readings

1. **Topology alone amplifies 1.06×; the real operator amplifies 27×.** The binary graph, with
   Dale's signs preserved and everything else discarded, has essentially no transient
   amplification. Forward-weight fraction falls from 0.995 to 0.716. This is the direct
   quantitative form of "the geometry lives in the weights, not the topology," and it is
   probably the single strongest number in the project.

2. **The base synaptic matrix is E/I-balanced.** Strip both scalings and E→I / I→E is 0.96 —
   symmetric. The ~900× feedforward asymmetry that the whole non-normality argument rests on is
   not in `c · g · ΔV · τ`. It is manufactured by `q` and `κ`.

3. **Ranking the sources: q > κ > wiring.** Removing the census weighting collapses peak
   amplification from 27.1 to 1.45; removing postsynaptic gain collapses it to 7.74. Population
   demography contributes more than excitability, and both contribute far more than the
   connectivity pattern.

## 3. The control that complicates it

Removing `q` from the operator is a *biological counterfactual* — "what if every population were
the same size." Rescaling the activity variable by `q` is something else entirely: a similarity
transform, which leaves the spectrum untouched but changes the transient.

Same operator, same eigenvalues, three activity variables:

| Activity variable | α(J) | ω(J) | Henrici | peak amp |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| extensive — A = population total output (the poster's A) | −0.0500 | 29.58 | 99.3% | **27.1×** |
| intensive — r = per-cell firing rate | −0.0500 | 62,700 | 100.0% | **47,695×** |
| symmetric split — A / √q | −0.0500 | 605 | 100.0% | **575×** |

Spectra agree to 4 × 10⁻¹⁵. Stability, α, the Euler-equivalence argument and every
eigenvalue-based claim are invariant. **Peak amplification, the numerical abscissa and the
Kreiss constant are not.**

So "27×" is not a property of the circuit. It is a property of the circuit *plus* a choice of
activity variable and norm, and the poster's extensive variable happens to give the **smallest**
amplification of the three natural choices.

### How to handle this rather than be ambushed by it

- State the norm explicitly, once, early: Euclidean norm on extensive population activity.
- Defend it physically. `A_i = q_i · r_i` is the total spike output of population *i*, which is
  what downstream populations actually receive and what contributes to LFP. The norm should
  measure what the network transmits, not what an electrode happens to sample from one cell.
  This is the same reasoning that fixes the energy norm in hydrodynamic stability.
- Report the table above anyway. Having it ready converts a damaging question into evidence of
  a firm grip on the geometry, and 27× is a conservative choice among the alternatives.
- Note that what is *qualitatively* invariant is the important part: Henrici departure is
  99.3–100% in every basis. "This operator is essentially fully non-normal" is robust; "it
  amplifies 27-fold" is conditional.

## 4. What this does to the thesis

The claim gets sharper and moves one level down:

> The hippocampal–entorhinal circuit's functionally feedforward geometry is not in its wiring.
> The synaptic substrate is close to E/I-balanced; the geometry is produced by the cell-type
> census and the postsynaptic excitability profile that the population model layers on top of it.

That is a stronger claim than the original, it is falsifiable, it is specific to this data, and
it is a direct argument for why Hippocampome's parameter tables — not just its connectivity —
are necessary to predict dynamics.

### Hypotheses it generates

**H1 — Amplification is set by E/I population-size imbalance.** Excitatory types average ~17×
more cells than inhibitory types, and removing that imbalance destroys 98% of the transient
gain. Prediction: circuits with more balanced E/I census show weaker non-normal amplification.
Testable across regions within this matrix, and across connectomes (C. elegans, hemibrain).

**H2 — Transient gain is state-dependent through κ.** `κ_j` is a linearisation slope, so it is a
function of the operating point, not a constant. Neuromodulators that differentially shift
interneuron versus principal-cell excitability — acetylcholine being the obvious candidate —
would retune the κ_I / κ_E ratio and therefore the feedforward asymmetry and the transient gain.
This turns a modelling caveat into an experimentally addressable prediction about
state-dependent amplification.

**H3 — A connectome alone cannot predict dynamics.** If the base synaptic matrix is
E/I-balanced and the geometry comes from census and excitability, then binary or even
synaptically-weighted connectomes are insufficient. This is a general methodological claim about
connectomics and a strong argument for the Hippocampome enterprise.

### Consequence for the GSN arm

Orbit counts are a *topological* featurisation, and topology alone carries little of the
dynamics here — so a constant-`edge_attr` run should be expected to do poorly, and that outcome
would **confirm** the thesis rather than refute the motif approach. The experiment cannot lose,
but the design should be three-way rather than two-way:

- (a) orbits only — pure topology
- (b) orbits + `q_i` and `κ_j` as node features — topology plus demography
- (c) full, with `m_ij` in the edge attribute

If (b) ≈ (c) ≫ (a), the result is that **motif structure becomes predictive once population size
and excitability are known**. That makes the 3-node motif load-bearing for the thesis instead of
parallel to it, which was the stated purpose of the GSN arm.

## 5. Caveats

- `q_i` was taken from `ml_qi_vector.csv`. Only `m_ij = w_ij · κ_j` is asserted at load; the
  further factorisation into `q · c · g · ΔV · τ` was **not** verified entry-by-entry. If that
  `q` is not exactly the one inside the `m_ij` product, the `M / q_i` row is approximate.
  Verify before quoting.
- The base row `M / (κ_j q_i)` is `c · g · ΔV · τ` only under the same assumption.
- Orderings come from a heuristic search, so `fwd weight` values are lower bounds on the true
  optimum. All five operators received identical treatment, so the comparison is fair even
  though the absolute numbers are not exact. The real-operator value (0.995) reproduces the
  repository's independently computed 0.9952.
- Peak amplification is a grid maximum over t ∈ (0, 8] at 160 points; the peak sits near
  t = 1.2, well inside the grid.
- The intensive transform is ill-conditioned by construction (`q` spans several orders of
  magnitude), so 47,695× is real but sensitive to the extremes of the census distribution.
- Everything here is at ρ = 0.95 and τ = 1, uniform.
