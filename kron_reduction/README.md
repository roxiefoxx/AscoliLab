# kron_reduction

What the 13 types missing from the 72-type list do to the operator. The 85 → 72 case is used
as a calibration experiment for the 37 types still missing between the 85-type operator and
the 122-type Hippocampome circuit.

**Which 72.** The 72-type list is an exact subset of the 85. The archived
`izhikevich_72_v*.csv` uses the same 72 types, except that one is spelled
"MEC LIII Multipolar Principal" where `w_ij_gaa` and the 85 have "MEC LV Multipolar Pyramidal".
The `w_ij_gaa` spelling is used here.

**The 13** (4 E, 9 I): CA1 Back Projection, CA1 Bistratified, CA1 O LM, CA1 Quadrilaminar,
CA2 Pyramidal (E), CA3 Basket CCK+, CA3 Horizontal Axo Axonic, CA3 Mossy Fiber Associated,
CA3 O LM, DG Hilar Ectopic Granule (E), EC LIII Pyramidal Stellate (E),
MEC LIII Superficial Multipolar Interneuron, SUB CA1 Projecting Pyramidal (E).

## Method

`W = M[post, pre]`, normalised once on the full 85 to ρ = 0.95; `J = −I + W`. Keep K (72),
eliminate R (13).

- **Truncation** `W_KK`: what a 72-type model is.
- **Static Kron** `W_KK + W_KR (I − W_RR)⁻¹ W_RK`: the Schur complement of `I − W`, exact at
  steady state. The correction is the sum over every path K → R → … → K. This is the same
  operation as the inhibitory `M_eff_E` in `inhib_modulation`, with a different set eliminated.
- **Dynamic Kron** `W_KK + W_KR ((1+s)I − W_RR)⁻¹ W_RK`: exact at every frequency.

Verified: static Kron reproduces `[(I − W)⁻¹]_KK` to 7 × 10⁻¹⁵, and dynamic Kron reproduces
`G(s)_KK` to ≤ 4 × 10⁻¹⁵. The full-85 summaries reproduce the published numbers
(27.13× peak, E→I/I→E 912, forward fraction 0.995185, Henrici 99.3%).

## Results (ρ_full = 0.95)

| | full 85 | truncated 72 | static Kron 72 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ρ(W) | 0.950 | **0.979** | 0.951 |
| stability margin 1 − ρ | 0.050 | **0.021** | 0.049 |
| steady-state error on the 72 | — | **0.72** | 7 × 10⁻¹⁵ |
| peak ‖e^{Jt}‖₂ (exact on the 72: 26.58) | 27.13 | 24.97 | 28.39 |
| Henrici (relative) | 0.993 | 0.994 | 0.994 |
| forward weight fraction (certified 85 order) | 0.9952 | 0.9959 | 0.9962 |
| E→I / I→E block ratio | 912 | **2,308** | 2,497 |
| edges | 1,641 | 1,151 | 2,933 |
| Dale wrong-sign entries (share of \|w\|) | 0 | 0 | 940 (0.034%) |

1. **Truncation moves the operating point.** Deleting the 13 types cuts the stability margin
   by a factor of 2.4. If the 85 is normalised to ρ = 0.98, the truncated 72 is unstable
   (ρ = 1.01). Steady-state responses on the 72 are 72% off. The ranking of responses to
   DG Granule drive survives (Spearman 0.977, 16% error). Renormalising the 72 to ρ = 0.95, as
   the project convention does, resets the margin by construction. A stand-alone 72-type
   study would therefore never see this effect.
2. **One type carries it.** MEC LIII Superficial Multipolar Interneuron alone moves ρ to 0.983
   when deleted, more than all 13 together. CA2 Pyramidal and EC LIII Pyramidal Stellate
   partly offset it in the opposite direction. The other 10 types change ρ by less than
   3 × 10⁻⁴. MEC LIII SMI has the largest right-eigenvector loading of the dominant mode
   (0.67). The left eigenvector is concentrated on EC LI II Multipolar Pyramidal (0.94), which
   gives SMI its strongest input (w = 14.8).
3. **Size is not impact.** CA2 Pyramidal's Kron correction is 21× larger in norm than MEC LIII
   SMI's, yet its effect on ρ is 23× smaller and has the opposite sign. The ranks still
   correlate overall (Spearman 0.90). The rank-1 first-order screen
   `δλ ≈ uᵀΔ_r v / uᵀv` matches the exact per-type shifts at r = 0.99999. It needs only the
   hidden type's in- and out-weights.
4. **It is about *which* types, not how many.** Against 1,000 random hidden sets with the same
   4 E / 9 I make-up, the observed ρ shift is unusual (p = 0.033). Only 16% of random sets raise ρ
   at all. Once a set is conditioned on containing MEC LIII SMI, the observed shift is
   unremarkable (p = 0.18), and 63% of those sets raise ρ. Neither the size of the correction
   (p = 0.46) nor the steady-state error (p = 0.28) is unusual.
5. **Geometry is robust; block ratios are not.** Henrici departure and forward weight fraction
   barely move. Peak amplification is bracketed: truncation is 6% low and static Kron 7% high.
   The E→I / I→E ratio jumps 2.5×, because the 9 hidden interneurons send 31% of all I→E
   weight. Under Kron reduction, that inhibition comes back as *negative E→E*, not as I→E:
   49% of the E→E correction weight is negative, i.e. disynaptic inhibition through hidden
   cells. A block ratio of a truncated or reduced operator measures which interneurons are
   parameterised.
6. **Static Kron is a steady-state method.** It beats truncation only below ω ≈ 1.04/τ. Above
   that it is worse, and it overshoots the early transient (peak 28.4 vs 26.6). The peak at
   t ≈ 1.2τ falls in exactly that band.
7. **Reduced operators break the orientation oracle.** `assert_post_pre` passes on the
   truncated 72, but raises on the Kron 72 in **both** orientations (count purity 0.575 / 0.782
   as is, 0.218 / 0.425 transposed). The wrong-sign entries carry only 0.034% of |w|.

## Imputation: what transfers to the 122

The 13 types' weights are thrown away. Their topology and Dale sign are kept, the weights are
imputed, and the result is Kron-reduced:

| method | steady-state error | ρ | share of ρ shift recovered |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| truncation | 0.720 | 0.979 | 0 |
| topology + E/I-block-median \|w\| | 0.693 | 0.979 | 0.02 |
| topology + known q_i, κ_j, block-median base B | **0.273** | **0.959** | **0.71** |
| oracle (true weights) | 0 | 0.951 | 1 |

Across the 1,000 null sets, `topology + q + κ` is the best method in 87% of sets and beats
truncation in 92%. Its median error is 0.27, against 0.46 for both truncation and block-median
imputation. Neither imputation recovers peak amplification (both give about 25×).

## What this suggests for the 122

The 85-type operator relates to the 122-type circuit as the 72 relates to the 85: it is a
truncation. The calibration suggests:

- **Treat 85-type absolutes as conditional on the 37 missing types.** That covers the
  stability margin, steady-state gains, the E→I/I→E ratio, and anything else that depends on
  the I→E block. A single unparameterised feedback interneuron can move them substantially.
  The geometric claims (essentially full non-normality, a ~0.995 forward weight fraction,
  peak amplification of about 25–28×) held up under this test. This strengthens the existing
  "ordinal claims only" caveat and points to which claims to lead with.
- **Only pass-through types matter.** A hidden type contributes to the Kron correction only if
  it both receives from and projects to the kept set. Pure sources and sinks contribute exactly
  zero. The first step for the 37 is to classify them from the 122-type potential-connectivity
  topology.
- **Screen before parameterising.** Imputed weights plus the first-order screen `uᵀΔ_r v / uᵀv`
  rank which of the 37 could move the dominant mode. That gives an evidence-based priority list
  for which types to parameterise first.
- **Collect census and excitability before wiring.** Topology alone recovered almost nothing;
  adding q and κ recovered most of the shift. This agrees with q > κ > wiring in
  `docs/WEIGHTS_VS_TOPOLOGY.md`.
- **Pick the reduction for the question.** Use static Kron for steady-state and gain questions.
  For transient amplification, use the frequency-dependent reduction, or keep the hidden types
  as explicit states with imputed parameters. Any reduced matrix needs a weight-share version
  of the orientation oracle.

## Caveats

- The 13 are not a random sample of the 37. They were added between two parameterisations,
  while the 37 are missing for lack of parameters and may be systematically different (for
  example, less characterised or rarer). The calibration gives scale and mechanism, not a
  prediction.
- Everything is linear and conditional on ρ = 0.95. The single-type effect grows sharply as
  the full operator approaches ρ = 1 (see the gain sweep).
- Imputation assumes the hidden types' topology is known exactly, which for the 37 means
  taking Hippocampome potential connectivity at face value.
